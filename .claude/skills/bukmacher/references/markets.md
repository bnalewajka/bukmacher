# Markets worth considering around 1.15–1.35, and their traps

Why several market types: at a fixed price the question is "which event, of all events
priced at 1.20, is truly the most likely?". A favourite's plain win at 1.20 can be less
likely than a total, a handicap or a double chance at the same price, because the second
kind of bet needs *two* things to go wrong. Look at all of them.

A price of 1.20 implies 83.3 %; after margin the book itself believes ~80 %. Your estimate
should be clearly above 83 % to propose it. At 1.15 you need >87 %, at 1.30 >77 %.

## Football
| Market | Typical use near 1.20 | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Double chance (1X / X2) | strong favourite away, or clear favourite where a draw is the main risk | a favourite that must win (cup, table race) may push and lose late; in dead rubbers both teams settle for the draw — fine for DC, bad for win bets |
| Draw no bet | favourite in a game where a draw is plausible; refund instead of loss | pays less than win; check which is offered — some books call AH 0 the same |
| Asian handicap +1 / +1.5 on favourite | favourite loses only if beaten by 2+ (AH +1: refund on a 1-goal loss) | reduced price for nothing if the favourite is huge; +1.5 in derbies is fine |
| Over 1.5 goals | both teams with attacking output, weak defences, high xG, big favourite at home | 0-0/1-0 in cagey cups, bad weather, defensive minnows parking the bus; check league goal average |
| Over 0.5 goals | lowest-variance total (≈1.05–1.10, so rarely in the 1.20 band) | only fits a lower target |
| Team to score / Under 4.5 / Under 5.5 | narrow use | low value, thin markets |
| Both teams to score: No | one team is toothless (0 shots on target streaks, xG < 0.6) | relies on one specific team being bad AND the favourite keeping a clean sheet |
| First half markets (1st half over 0.5 etc.) | rarely in band | skip unless clear reason |
| "To qualify" (cups, two legs) | different from 1X2 — includes extra time/penalties | verify the market definition; a team leading 3-0 from the 1st leg is priced ~1.05 |

Football facts to remember: home favourites at ~1.20 in top leagues win ≈78–82 % of the time;
away favourites at the same price win less often than the price suggests; cup games and
international friendlies see heavy rotation; the last 10 minutes are where 1.20 dies.

## Basketball
| Market | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Moneyline (incl. OT) | big favourite | NBA favourites rest stars (load management, back-to-back), tanking teams occasionally win; NBA moneyline at 1.20 corresponds to ~7–8 pt spread |
| Handicap for the underdog (+10.5, +15.5) or favourite -x.5 at 1.20 | more control over the risk | garbage time and late fouls decide the margin randomly; alternate lines in the 1.20 band are usually poor value |
| Team total / game total alt lines | rarely in band | pace-dependent |
| First half / first quarter winner | favourite starts strong | starters rotate; variance higher than full game |
| Euroleague / domestic leagues | favourite in a league with big gaps (e.g. top team vs bottom team) | check whether the favourite has a Euroleague game within 2 days (rotation), and injury notes from the club |

Basketball note: overtime is included in totals/handicap/moneyline unless the book says
"regular time". Check the rule at the actual bookmaker.

## Ice hockey
| Market | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Moneyline **including OT/SO** ("2-way") | favourite; loses only if beaten | at 1.20 there are almost no NHL favourites (parity); European leagues yes |
| 3-way "60 minutes" | avoid for favourites — a tie is a loss | the most common way a hockey "sure thing" loses |
| Puck line +1.5 for favourite | favourite loses only by 2+ goals | empty-net goals inflate margins — a +1.5 on the *underdog* dies to empty-netters; on the favourite it is safer |
| Over 4.5 / Under 7.5 total | pace and goalies | starting goalie decides everything; confirmed goalies appear only hours before puck drop |
| Team total over 1.5 | strong offence vs weak goalie | shutouts happen (~7 % of NHL games) |

## Tennis
| Market | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Match winner | top player vs qualifier/lucky loser; big ranking gap; favourable surface | *retirement rules*: some books void if a player retires before set 1 completes, others settle — check; injured favourites (taped legs, MTOs last match) lose or retire; first-round after a long trip / after a title week; lucky losers and qualifiers are match-tight; women's tennis has more upsets at 1.20 than men's |
| Set handicap −1.5 (favourite wins in straight sets) | only when priced in band (very lopsided) | one tie-break goes wrong and the bet is dead |
| Set handicap +1.5 for the underdog / "favourite wins at least one set" | rarely in band, but structurally safest | thin liquidity |
| Total games over/under alt lines | rarely | no |

Tennis is where "predictions" are most unreliable and where recent-form fitness news matters most:
check last match duration, medical time-outs, withdrawals in the last month, doubles played
today, order of play (late night session after rain delays).

## Volleyball
| Market | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Match winner | strong club vs bottom team; national team vs minnow in NL/qualifiers | best-of-5 sets reduces variance, but rotation in dead-rubber tournament games is common; check who travels |
| Set handicap −1.5 (3-0 or 3-1) | lopsided | 3-2 loses it |
| Set handicap +1.5 / "wins at least one set" | underdog with a decent block | thin |
| Total sets under 4.5 | very lopsided | same as −1.5 |
| Tournaments (Nations League, Worlds, CEV) | national team form and squad depth; second-string squads in group stage | look at the actual roster announced, not the country's reputation |

Volleyball data is thinnest of the five; one-sided leagues (PlusLiga top vs bottom, Italian
SuperLega, Turkish, Russian) do have real 1.15–1.25 favourites.

## Cross-sport traps
- **Rule definitions differ per bookmaker**: OT included or not, retirement rules, abandoned
  matches, "regular time". Say which rule the proposal assumes.
- **Line shopping**: the same bet can be 1.18 at one book and 1.25 at another; quote the
  bookmaker and the time.
- **Drift**: if the price has *lengthened* since opening (`drift > 0`), the market learned
  something — find out what before proposing. Shortening (`drift < 0`) is mild confirmation.
- **Overround on favourites**: soft books put most of the margin on the favourite; that is
  why 1.20 is often "true" 1.25.
- **Correlated risk**: proposals are singles; do not stack bets from the same event.
